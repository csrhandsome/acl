\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced \bench{}, a cross-embodiment tactile dataset and frozen representation benchmark.
By mapping both articulated dexterous hands and two-finger parallel grippers onto a canonical MANO UV atlas under a fixed-scale, validity-aware data contract, \bench{} enables apples-to-apples evaluation of frozen tactile encoders across information retention (L0), proxy, physical, and temporal readability (L1), and cross-domain generalization (L2).
Together with our controlled suite of reconstruction and latent-prediction tactile encoders, \bench{} provides a reproducible foundation for studying what tactile representations capture and how well they transfer across robot embodiments.

\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

First, uncalibrated UV intensities represent fixed-scale contact intensity or depth proxies rather than calibrated Newton forces; cross-source gray levels are not directly comparable in absolute physical units, and L1b physical force evaluation requires verified external wrench labels.
Second, kinematic retargeting for dexterous hands incurs non-zero fingertip alignment residuals, and current dexterous-hand exports lack per-source validity masks (whereas parallel-gripper reference frames are automated candidates and right-hand correspondence remains diagnostic; see Appendix~\ref{app:audit} for audit details).
Finally, \bench{} currently focuses on offline frozen-representation probing; physics consistency (L3) and closed-loop manipulation control (L4) remain important directions for future work, and full six-source test evaluations are ongoing.

\section*{Ethics Statement}
All tactile datasets unified in this work are derived from publicly available or institutionally authorized robotic manipulation corpora containing no personally identifiable human subject data.
Our retargeting and benchmarking software introduces no direct dual-use safety hazards; however, deploying uncalibrated tactile proxies in safety-critical physical human-robot interaction without hardware-level force calibration could cause unintended contact forces, which we explicitly caution against in our data contract and limitations.
